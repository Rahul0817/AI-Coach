# Before Reading Any Result

Reference ranges differ between laboratories because they depend on the assay
used and the population sampled. Always compare a value against the range
printed on your own report, not against a range from the internet. Timing also
matters: several of these hormones vary across the cycle, and most are measured
on days 2 to 5 of a cycle for that reason.

A single out-of-range value is not a diagnosis. Interpretation depends on the
whole panel, your symptoms, and your clinical history.

# Androgens

**Total testosterone** is often mildly elevated in PCOS. Values markedly above
the reference range warrant investigation for other causes, including androgen
secreting tumours and congenital adrenal hyperplasia.

**Free testosterone** and the **free androgen index** (total testosterone
divided by SHBG, multiplied by 100) are frequently more informative than total
testosterone. High insulin suppresses SHBG, so free androgen can be elevated
while total testosterone reads as normal — a very common pattern in PCOS.

**SHBG** (sex hormone binding globulin) is often low. Low SHBG is itself a
marker of insulin resistance.

**DHEA-S** is an adrenal androgen. It is moderately raised in a subset of
people with PCOS. Markedly high values point toward an adrenal source and
require specialist assessment.

**Androstenedione** is sometimes measured as an additional androgen marker.

# Gonadotropins

**LH** (luteinising hormone) and **FSH** (follicle-stimulating hormone) are
usually measured together in the early follicular phase. An elevated LH:FSH
ratio — often cited around 2:1 or higher — is a classic PCOS pattern, though
it is present in only a proportion of cases and is no longer part of the formal
diagnostic criteria. A normal ratio does not exclude PCOS.

**AMH** (anti-Müllerian hormone) reflects the number of small follicles and is
typically elevated in PCOS. It is increasingly discussed as a possible
substitute for ultrasound, but assay standardisation remains a limitation.
Elevated AMH indicates a high follicle count, not high fertility.

**Prolactin** is measured to exclude hyperprolactinaemia, which causes
irregular cycles through a different mechanism and has a different treatment.

# Metabolic Panel

**Fasting glucose** is often normal even when insulin resistance is
significant, because the pancreas compensates. A normal fasting glucose does
not rule out insulin resistance.

**Fasting insulin** is more sensitive as an early marker. It is not
standardised across laboratories, which limits comparison between reports.

**HOMA-IR** is calculated from fasting glucose and fasting insulin and is
widely used in research as an insulin-resistance index.

**HbA1c** reflects average glucose over roughly the preceding three months and
is the standard screen for prediabetes and diabetes.

**Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)** with a 75g glucose load is the most
sensitive routine test for impaired glucose tolerance, and current PCOS
guidance recommends it in preference to fasting glucose alone for many people.

**Lipid panel** — total cholesterol, LDL, HDL and triglycerides. A raised
triglyceride level with a low HDL is a characteristic pattern in insulin
resistance. The triglyceride-to-HDL ratio is a useful derived marker.

# Thyroid and Other Exclusions

**TSH**, and where indicated free T4, are checked because hypothyroidism
produces overlapping symptoms — irregular cycles, weight gain, fatigue, hair
thinning — and is straightforward to treat.

**17-hydroxyprogesterone** is measured to exclude non-classical congenital
adrenal hyperplasia, which can closely mimic PCOS.

**Vitamin D** is commonly low in PCOS. Deficiency is worth correcting on
general health grounds.

# What to Bring to an Appointment

A clinician can do considerably more with three months of logged cycle dates,
symptom patterns and any previous lab reports than with a verbal summary. If
you have used this app to track, export your data and take it with you. Ask
which tests are being ordered and why, and ask for a copy of the results —
having your own record makes every subsequent appointment more productive.
